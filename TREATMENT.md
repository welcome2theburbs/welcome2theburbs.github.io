# Welcome 2 The 'Burbs

*Fan-written sequel to* The 'Burbs *(1989). Not affiliated with the original film or its rights holders.*

## Premise
Thirty-odd years after the Klopek incident, Mayfield Place has gone quiet. The old crowd is gone, but two kids who watched it all go down are back, and something has moved into the house everyone swore they'd never look at again.

## Main characters

**RICKY BUTLER** (56) — Was the teenage binoculars-and-popcorn instigator of the block. Now a recently divorced, slightly deflated man flying in to clear out his late parents' house. Still the guy who notices things. Still wrong about half of them. The other half is the problem.

**DAVE PETERSON** (50, just turned) — Ray's son, and the younger of the two by six years, which Ricky still holds over him. His parents (Ray and Carol) retired to a warmer climate and left him the house, which he's "just keeping an eye on." He is the sensible one, mostly because he grew up watching what happens when you aren't. Works remotely, owns a lot of lawn equipment, is trying very hard to be normal.

**LT. MARK RUMSFIELD, ret.** (90s) — The last original neighbor. Still on the block, still in the flag-and-fatigues business, impossibly old and sharp as ever. Technically cared for by Connie.

**CONNIE** (late 20s) — Rumsfield's live-in caregiver, sweet-voiced, always carrying a thermos of tea, and quietly the smartest person on the street. Everyone on the block assumes she's his very young wife and gives her a look; she's too polite (and too professionally trained) to correct them more than twice. Rumsfield, who has opinions, refuses to clarify. She notices the Ostranders' perfect lawn before anyone else does, and she is not as surprised by them as she should be. Her name is a nod to Bonnie, Rumsfield's previous (also much younger) wife.

**RUSS DUFFY** (late 50s) — Dave's neighbor on the other side, and the block's new Art-shaped hole: loud, cheerful, perpetually between jobs, always in the middle of a snack. Not Art, but the same species. He turns up uninvited with a six-pack and a worse idea, treats Dave's fridge as his own, and has a doorbell camera feed that he insists is "basically evidence." He is the first to say what everyone is thinking, usually in the wrong tone and at the wrong volume. Whether he's a friend, a liability, or both is the open question (it's both).

## The new neighbors
Replacing the Klopeks: **the OSTRANDERS** (also called "the Ostrander Household" — they insist on "household," not "family").

- Six or seven adults of unclear relation, moving in over a single night.
- The house, which used to be a rotting wreck, is now *immaculate*. Fresh paint, perfect lawn, no furnace smell. This is far more unsettling.
- Nobody has ever seen all of them in the same room.
- Leader-ish figure: **DR. ODILE OSTRANDER** (60s), warm, serene, tote bag, offers everyone a casserole.
- Genuine question the script plays with: wellness cult, witness protection, a very private startup, or the actual thing Ricky and Dave think it is? (Decide in Act Two. See Open Questions.)

Name alternatives if "Ostrander" doesn't land: **the Vandekamps, the Mortensens, the Pruitt-Vaskos, the Hollowbecks, the Zielinskis.**

## Tone
Suburban paranoia comedy with real dread underneath. Lean on the original's rhythm: nothing is happening, then everything is happening on a lawn. Jokes come from people being petty in the face of possible horror.

## Structure sketch (rough)
1. **Act One** — Ricky arrives. Dave is house-sitting. Rumsfield has a very young caregiver and the same old suspicions. Russ shows up with a six-pack and a theory. The Ostranders move in overnight. Dave refuses to get involved. Ricky is already involved.
2. **Act Two** — Surveillance, bad ideas, a trash-can incident. Dave is pulled in when something of his goes missing. Connie reveals she knows more than she says. The Ostranders host a block party. Everyone attends. Nobody leaves normal.
3. **Act Three** — Basement. There is always a basement.

## Open questions
- What are the Ostranders, really? (Harmless? Dangerous? Both?)
- Who is Connie really, and how did she end up as Rumsfield's caregiver? (Who hired her, and why does she know so much?)
- Is Russ Duffy a descendant, in-law or just a spiritual cousin of Art Weingartner, or does the film never say?
- What happened to Bonnie? Ricky and Dave would absolutely bring her up, and Connie might have an opinion.
- Does Ricky's inventory of his parents' house (boxes, secrets) tie into the Klopek past?
- Cameos / references to Art, Ray, Carol, Walter: dead, moved, or off camera?
- Do the original actors appear, or is it fully re-cast?
